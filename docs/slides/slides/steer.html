About 50 seconds. This is where I put in the work. Strategy first. The site floors corr² to the nearest tenth, so only bucket jumps pay, and I set targets that way: G3 to 800, G4 to 900, the total past 2700, and no upload unless it should clear 0.7. When the board showed teams at 2720, I read that as our score plus a perfect G2 and sent the last push there, at G2’s MPS truncation. I also designed the team: one strategist, one runner, and one gateway to BlueQubit with a spend ledger and a $2 cap. On the technical side, I pushed the noise angle: treat Pauli-path truncation like noise, which led to Richardson builds from each qubit’s finest runs, with the fit chosen on held-out runs. Pushing G4 to 900 is what exposed that the threshold floor is set per qubit, so 20 qubits could run finer. And I had us pressure-test real hardware: at 2,000 to 7,300 two-qubit gates the signal doesn’t survive the noise, so we stayed classical. The agents gave me speed; the calls were mine.
